# Explore Civic Accord in Ten Minutes

Civic Accord is a **prototype with fictional bills and districts**. Use it to learn the interface and ask better questions. Its estimates and scores are not official evidence or advice.

1. **Choose a bill.** In [Bills](https://governanceapp.ai-aarti.com/bills), search the three sample records and open one. Read each section summary. Treat the source cards as demonstration content; they do not link to verified original documents.
2. **Change the district.** In [Impact](https://governanceapp.ai-aarti.com/impact), select the same bill and compare two district profiles. The displayed percentage is a formula-based exposure equivalent. It is not a measured benefit or prediction.
3. **Try the priority sliders.** In [Voter feedback](https://governanceapp.ai-aarti.com/feedback), select a preset group. One vote costs one token, two cost four, and three cost nine. The page displays a 50-token budget; it allows overspending and does not submit or save your changes.
4. **Compare options.** In [Compromise analysis](https://governanceapp.ai-aarti.com/compromise), inspect the preset amendments and the lowest group utility for each. The recommendation comes from assumed numbers, not a poll, negotiation, or legal review.
5. **Check the inputs.** In [Stakeholder trust](https://governanceapp.ai-aarti.com/trust), see how four stored attributes make up each sample score. The scores do not change the recommendation.

If you want to discuss a real bill, first find its current text and status through an authoritative legislative source. Identify who may be affected, ask how any estimate was produced, and clearly separate your values from measured facts. Civic Accord does not contact representatives, collect votes, or track enacted outcomes for you.

The [worked example](how-the-governance-app-works.md) explains each screen in more depth. The [methods guide](pareto-governance-engine-and-math.md) shows the formulas.
